I’m a data visualization designer based in Berlin, with a background in architecture and urban research. I work on the whole path from raw tables to a published story: cleaning and modelling the data, analysing it, and designing the charts, maps and dashboards people read.

I trained in architecture and worked in urban research and design, studying how people use buildings and cities. Questions about housing, public space and who has access to them led me to data, and to learning the tools to answer them myself.

Design gives structure to my analytical process. It helps me think visually and systemically, not just about how data looks, but how it’s understood. I approach datasets like design problems, organizing complexity into clear, meaningful forms.

I aim to make complexity understandable and engaging for both technical and non-technical audiences. Clarity, narrative, and structure are key to turning data into insights that support informed decisions.

Data is a powerful way to understand how systems function and change. I enjoy finding patterns that connect analysis to real human stories, when numbers become insight and they materialize graphically.
